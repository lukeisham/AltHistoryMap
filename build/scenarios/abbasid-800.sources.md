# Abbasid Caliphate AD 800: sources for the country key facts

Every value below was found by one researcher and confirmed against a source by a second, independent checker. A field no source supported is left blank in the scenario.

## Colour 1: Abbasid Caliphate (central provinces)

State: Abbasid Caliphate under Harun al-Rashid, AD 800 (central provinces).

- **ruler**: Harun al-Rashid, Caliph and Commander of the Faithful. Check: verified. Evidence: Tabari vol. XXX (archive.org djvu text), events of 170 AH: allegiance given to Harun as Caliph on his brother al-Hadi's death; the accession speech calls the successor Commander of the Faithful. Brentjes/McGill: reigned 786-809, which covers 800. Cited: The History of al-Tabari, Vol. XXX: The 'Abbasid Caliphate in Equilibrium (A.D. 785-809/A.H. 169-193), C. E. Bosworth (trans. and annot.), State University of New York Press, Albany, 1989 (full text read at archive.org/stream/tabarivolume30/Tabari_Volume_30_djvu.txt), Events of the year 170 (A.H.), 'Harun's Assumption of the Caliphate' (contents list p. 91); text: 'Allegiance was given to al-Rashid Harun b. Muhammad b. 'Abdallah b. al-'Abbas as Caliph'; speech there styles him the Commander of the Faithful (approximate locator)

- **government**: Hereditary Islamic caliphate. Check: unsupported; trimmed to what the check confirmed (claimed: Hereditary Islamic caliphate, absolute monarchy). Evidence: EB 1911 Caliphate (Wikisource): sons succeed fathers, and Harun's deed fixes succession of Amin, Mamun, Qasim, so hereditary is supported. 'Stood far above all his subjects' is said of Mansur's time and the phrase 'absolute monarchy' is not used for 800. Cited: Encyclopaedia Britannica, 11th ed., 'Caliphate' (Vol. 5), M. J. de Goeje, https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Caliphate (transcription of the 1911 edition), Section C.2 'Reign of Mansur' (caliph 'stood far above all his subjects'; son al-Mahdi succeeds) and C.5 'Reign of Harun al-Rashid' (vizier Yahya; sons named in order of succession)

- **currency**: Dinar (gold) and dirham (silver). Check: verified. Evidence: Khalili catalogue: gold dinar of 182 AH (798-9) and silver dirham of 193 AH (808-9), both attributed to Harun's reign; together they show both denominations in issue around 800. Cited: Abbasid gold dinar, NM 182 AH, temp. Harun al-Rashid, Acc. AB507 (ref. Bernardi 2010 no. 51), Khalili Collections (museum catalogue), https://www.khalilicollections.org/islamic-coins/9657-abbasid-gold-dinar-nm-182-ah/, Item 9657: AU dinar, 4.15 g, 182 AH | Abbasid silver dirham, Tabaristan, 193 AH, temp. Harun al-Rashid, Acc. AR0747 (ref. SCC no. 1100), Khalili Collections (museum catalogue), https://www.khalilicollections.org/islamic-coins/2049-abbasid-silver-dirham-tabaristan-193-ah/, Item 2049: AR dirham, 2.87 g, 193 AH

- **demonym**: Abbasid. Check: verified. Evidence: Brentjes entry (Springer/McGill) styles Harun's realm the 'Abbasid Empire and him caliph 786-809; Tabari also names Harun b. Muhammad b. Abdallah b. al-Abbas. Cited: Hārūn al-Rashīd, in The Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers, Sonja Brentjes, Springer, New York, 2007, pp. 474-475, DOI 10.1007/978-0-387-30400-7_586 (read at islamsci.mcgill.ca/RASI/BEA/Harun_al-Rashid_BEA.htm), entry text: caliph of the 'Abbasid empire 786-809
