# Roman Empire AD 117: sources for the country key facts

Every value below was found by one researcher and confirmed against a source by a second, independent checker. A field no source supported is left blank in the scenario.

## Colour 1: Roman Empire

State: Roman Empire under Trajan, then Hadrian.

- **ruler**: Trajan, Roman Emperor (Augustus). Check: verified. Evidence: Dio 68.28-33 (opened): Trajan, hailed imperator, campaigns in Mesopotamia in 116-117 and dies at Selinus after a reign of 19 years 6 months 15 days (from 98 = Aug 117). Dio does not itself use the title Augustus; the ANU catalogue entry (opened) shows the AVG title on his denarius legend. Debevoise p. 239 (archive.org text) puts his death early August 117, so he ruled for most of 117. Cited: Roman History, Book 68 (Loeb text), Cassius Dio, https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/68*.html (LacusCurtius, Loeb Classical Library text), 68.28-30 and 68.33

- **government**: Principate: an emperor ruling with senate and magistrates. Check: verified. Evidence: Cited Res Gestae ch. 34 (opened) concerns Augustus returning the state to senate and people, i.e. the founding period, not 117 on its own. For Trajan's day: Dio 68.5.2 and 68.7.3 (opened) show emperor dealing with the senate, 68.25.1 names a consul; ANU denarius (opened) shows Trajan consul VI, with SPQR and Pontifex Maximus. Magistrate evidence is modest. Cited: Res Gestae Divi Augusti (Loeb 1924), Augustus, https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Augustus/Res_Gestae/6*.html (LacusCurtius, Loeb text), chapter 34

- **currency**: Denarius (silver). Check: verified. Evidence: ANU Classics Museum entry (opened): silver denarius of Trajan, 2.769 g, dated AD 116, one year before the scenario, issued at Rome. Cited: Denarius of Trajan, AD 116 (acc. 1965.07), catalogue entry, Beryl Rawson (catalogue author), ANU Classics Museum, https://slll.cass.anu.edu.au/classics-museum/catalogue/objects/denarius-trajan-196507, catalogue entry; cites Mattingly, BMCRE III, 578

- **demonym**: Roman. Check: verified. Evidence: Dio 68.28-33 (opened) calls the people of the empire Romans throughout the Parthian campaign and Trajan's death. Cited: Roman History, Book 68 (Loeb text), Cassius Dio, https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/68*.html (LacusCurtius, Loeb Classical Library text), 68.28-30 and 68.33

## Colour 3: Parthian Empire

State: Parthian Empire (Arsacid dynasty).

- **ruler**: Osroes I, King of Kings. Check: verified. Evidence: Debevoise (opened text of the same book): king table gives Osroes ca. 109/10-128/29; p. 237 names him the great king driven from Ctesiphon 116; p. 239 says Parthians rejected Parthamaspates and Osroes seems to have resumed control in spring 117 ('some indication'). Title King of Kings per p. xxxviii. Rival Vologases ruled the east. Smith's Dictionary (Perseids, opened) also says Chosroes was restored after Trajan died in 117. Cited: A Political History of Parthia, Neilson C. Debevoise, University of Chicago Press, 1938; https://isac-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/isac-publications/political_history_parthia.pdf, Table of Parthian kings, 'Osroes ca. 109/10-128/29'; pp. 216, 239, 242

- **government**: Hereditary monarchy: King of Kings over vassal kings. Check: verified. Evidence: Debevoise (opened text): p. xxxviii calls the empire a feudatory pyramid with the King of Kings at the apex; p. xl says vassal kings remained in Adiabene, Characene, Elymais, Persis, Hatra, Osroene; the Arsacid family kept the succession to the throne. The PDF link itself would not decode, so the archive.org text of the same book was read. Cited: A Political History of Parthia, Neilson C. Debevoise, University of Chicago Press, 1938; https://isac-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/isac-publications/political_history_parthia.pdf, Introduction, p. xxxviii (empire as a 'vast feudatory power', apex the King of Kings)

- **currency**: Drachm (silver). Check: verified. Evidence: Harvard Art Museums record (opened): silver (AR) drachm of Vologases III, Ecbatana, dated 105-147 CE, a range that includes 117. Cited: Drachm of Vologases III of Parthia, 1976.79.1231 (Sellwood 78.7), Harvard Art Museums / Arthur M. Sackler Museum, https://www.harvardartmuseums.org/collections/object/181020, catalogue record: silver (AR), 3.16 g, Ecbatana, 105-147 CE

- **demonym**: Parthian. Check: verified. Evidence: Dio 68.30.3 and 68.33.2 (opened) call the people Parthians, including those who rejected Parthamaspates in 117. Cited: Roman History, Book 68 (Loeb text), Cassius Dio, https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/68*.html (LacusCurtius, Loeb Classical Library text), 68.28-30 and 68.33
