# British Empire 1920: sources for the country key facts

Every value below was found by one researcher and confirmed against a source by a second, independent checker. A field no source supported is left blank in the scenario.

## Colour 1: British Empire (United Kingdom, colonies, protectorates)

State: United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland with the Crown colonies, protectorates and condominium (Egypt, Sudan, Nigeria, Kenya, Straits Settlements, etc.).

- **ruler**: George V, King of the United Kingdom. Check: verified. Evidence: London Gazette notice (opened): the 1901 style, King of the UK and of the British dominions beyond the seas, was first used by George V at his 1910 accession. George V reigned 1910-36. Cited: The London Gazette: 'Royal Titles' notice on the proclamation of 4 November 1901 (supplement, issue 27371), The Gazette (official public record), http://www.thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/content/104132, text of the notice: 'The amended title was first used at an Accession Council in May 1910 when King George V came to the throne'

- **government**: Crown colonies ruled by governors. Check: unsupported; trimmed to what the check confirmed (claimed: Constitutional monarchy; colonies ruled by Crown governors). Evidence: Britannica 1911 (opened): in crown colonies the crown controls legislation through a governor and crown-appointed council. It never calls the UK a constitutional monarchy; royal.uk role-of-monarchy page (opened) says so, undated, not for 1920. Cited: Encyclopaedia Britannica, 11th ed., 'British Empire', Cambridge University Press (1910-11), https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/British_Empire, section 'Administration'

## Colour 2: British Empire (self-governing Dominions)

State: The self-governing Dominions under the Crown: Canada, Australia, South Africa and New Zealand (Newfoundland may also be painted).

- **ruler**: George V, King of the British Dominions beyond the Seas. Check: verified. Evidence: Gazette notice (opened): the 1901 Royal Titles Act added 'British dominions beyond the seas' to the royal style; George V first used it in 1910. Cited: The London Gazette: 'Royal Titles' notice on the proclamation of 4 November 1901 (supplement, issue 27371), The Gazette (official public record), http://www.thegazette.co.uk/all-notices/content/104132, text of the notice: 'The amended title was first used at an Accession Council in May 1910 when King George V came to the throne'

- **government**: Self-governing dominions under a constitutional monarchy. Check: verified. Evidence: Constitution Act 1867 (opened): Canada a Dominion under the Crown with a constitution similar in principle to the UK's; executive power vested in the monarch. Britannica 1911 (opened): dominions have cabinet government responsible to parliament, crown keeps only a veto. Cited: Constitution Act, 1867 (British North America Act), UK Parliament; Government of Canada, https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/page-1.html, Preamble; s.9

- **demonym**: British subject. Check: verified. Evidence: British Nationality and Status of Aliens Act 1914 (opened), s.1(1)(a): a person born within the King's dominions and allegiance is a natural-born British subject. In force in 1920. Cited: British Nationality and Status of Aliens Act 1914 (4 & 5 Geo. 5 c. 17), UK Parliament, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/4-5/17/enacted, section 1(1)(a)

## Colour 3: British Empire (League of Nations mandates)

State: Territories held by Britain under League of Nations mandates in 1920 (Palestine, Iraq, Tanganyika; South West Africa through the Union of South Africa).

- **ruler**: George V, King (His Britannic Majesty, the Mandatory). Check: verified. Evidence: Palestine Mandate preamble (opened, 1922 text) says the Allied Powers selected His Britannic Majesty as Mandatory; the selection dates from San Remo, April 1920 (Jewish Virtual Library, opened). George V was king in 1920. Cited: Mandate for Palestine (League of Nations, 24 July 1922), League of Nations, https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/palmanda.asp, Preamble ('selected His Britannic Majesty as the Mandatory') and Article 1

- **government**: British mandate under the League of Nations. Check: verified. Evidence: Covenant Art. 22 (opened): advanced nations guard former Ottoman and German territories as Mandatories on behalf of the League. Britain was allocated Palestine and Iraq at San Remo, 1920 (opened). Cited: Covenant of the League of Nations, Article 22, League of Nations (Paris Peace Conference, 1919), https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/leagcov.asp, Article 22

## Colour 4: British Empire (British India)

State: British India (the Indian Empire under the Viceroy), including the territory under the Government of India.

- **ruler**: George V, King and Emperor of India. Check: verified. Evidence: NZ Gazette 1902 (opened): the 1901 proclamation kept 'Emperor of India' in the royal style. Gazette notice (opened): George V used that style from 1910. royal.uk (opened) calls George V King-Emperor on his 1911 India visit. Cited: New Zealand Gazette 1902, issue 7: Proclamation under the Royal Titles Act, 1901 (reprint of the 4 Nov 1901 proclamation), Government of New Zealand, https://gazette.howison.co.nz/nzg/1902/issue/7/page/156, page 156

- **government**: Crown rule by the Viceroy and Governor-General in Council. Check: verified. Evidence: Britannica 1911 (opened): supreme executive power in India rests with the viceroy and council, under the Secretary of State in London. Cited: Encyclopaedia Britannica, 11th ed., 'British Empire', Cambridge University Press (1910-11), https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/British_Empire, section 'Administration'

- **currency**: Indian rupee. Check: verified. Evidence: RBI Museum page (opened): the Rupee One note of November 1917 bore George V's portrait; small notes were issued until January 1926, so the rupee was in use in 1920. Cited: Reserve Bank of India Museum: British India paper notes, Reserve Bank of India, https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/pm_britishindia.aspx, section 'Small Denomination Notes' (Re 1 note of 1917 with George V portrait, issued to 1926)

- **demonym**: Indian. Check: verified. Evidence: Government of India Act 1919 edition (opened, first 100,000 characters): the preamble speaks of Indians in every branch of administration, using 'Indian' for the people. Cited: The Government of India Act 1919 (9 & 10 Geo. 5 c. 101), ed. H. N. Mitra, 'The Government of India Act 1919, Rules Thereunder & Government Reports, 1920', Parliament of the UK; ed. H. N. Mitra, Annual Register Office, Calcutta, 1921; https://archive.org/stream/govtofindiaact19029669mbp/govtofindiaact19029669mbp_djvu.txt, Preamble ('Indians in every branch of Indian administration'); s.1 (Governor-General in Council) (approximate locator)
