# Mongol Empire 1279: sources for the country key facts

Every value below was found by one researcher and confirmed against a source by a second, independent checker. A field no source supported is left blank in the scenario.

## Colour 1: Yuan Empire (China)

State: Yuan dynasty of Kublai, which completed the conquest of Song China in 1279.

- **ruler**: Kublai (Shizu), Emperor of the Yuan and Great Khan. Check: verified. Evidence: Yuanshi Benji 10 (opened), headed Shizu 7, has a Zhiyuan 16 (1279) year section with the emperor (di) acting, including the Yashan end of the Song. Iranica 'Abaqa' (opened) calls Qubilai the Great Khan in China, Abaqa's suzerain. Cited: Yuanshi (History of the Yuan), Benji 10: Shizu 7, entry for the 16th year of Zhiyuan (1279), Song Lian et al. (compilers), https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/元史/卷010, 本紀第十 世祖七, 至元十六年 (Yashan entry; the ruler appears as 帝)

- **government**: Imperial monarchy under a Mongol dynasty. Check: verified. Evidence: Yuanshi Benji 7 (opened): in the eleventh month of Zhiyuan 8 the court proclaims the dynastic name Great Yuan; Iranica 'Mongols' (opened) says the Mongol emperors of China ruled until Ming expelled them in 1368. Cited: Yuanshi, Benji 7: Shizu 4, 8th year of Zhiyuan (1271), eleventh month: edict founding the dynastic name Great Yuan, Song Lian et al. (compilers), https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/元史/卷007, 本紀第七 世祖四, 至元八年十一月 (建國號曰大元)

- **currency**: Zhongtong paper note (Zhongtong chao). Check: verified. Evidence: Yuanshi Zhi 42 (opened), paper-money issuance table: Zhiyuan 16 (1279) lists only Zhongtong notes; Zhiyuan notes first appear in the table at Zhiyuan 24 (1287). Cited: Yuanshi, Zhi 42 (Shihuo 1), section on paper money (Chaofa), Song Lian et al. (compilers), https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/元史/卷093, 卷九十三 志第四十二 食貨一, 鈔法 (中統元年 notes; Zhiyuan notes only from the 24th year of Zhiyuan, 1287)

## Colour 2: Golden Horde (Ulus of Jochi)

State: Golden Horde (Ulus of Jochi) under Mongke Temur.

- **ruler**: Mongke Temur, Khan of the Golden Horde. Check: verified. Evidence: Hope, BSOAS 88/1 (opened on Cambridge Core, pp. 49-69) calls the Jochid prince Mongke Temur reigning 1269-80; Iranica 'Abaqa' (opened) calls Mengu Temur the new khan of the Golden Horde after Berke. Page 58 not separately confirmed. Cited: An ulus within an ulus: the afterlife of Ariq Boke's appanage in the Mongol Empire (1252-1336), Michael Hope, Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African Studies 88/1 (2025), pp. 49-69, doi:10.1017/S0041977X24000612, p. 58 ('the Jochid prince Mongke Temur (r. 1269-80)')

- **government**: Mongol khanate (Jochid ulus). Check: verified. Evidence: Iranica 'Golden Horde' (opened) opens by defining it as the Mongol khanate ruled by Joci's descendants. Cited: Golden Horde, Peter Jackson, Encyclopaedia Iranica XI/1, pp. 75-76, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/golden-horde/, opening paragraph ('the Mongol Khanate ruled by the descendants of Joci')

## Colour 3: Chagatai Khanate

State: Chagatai Khanate under the overlordship of Qaidu.

- **government**: Mongol khanate under Qaidu's overlordship. Check: verified. Evidence: Iranica 'Chaghatay Dynasty i. History' (opened): Qaidu, made Great Khan about 1271, held the Chagatai ulus in a stranglehold for over three decades, so it covers 1279. Cited: Chaghatay Dynasty i. History, Peter Jackson, Encyclopaedia Iranica V/4, pp. 343-346, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chaghatay-dynasty/i-history/, paragraphs on Qaidu ('exercised a stranglehold upon Chagatai's ulus for over three decades')

## Colour 4: Ilkhanate

State: Ilkhanate of Persia under Abaqa.

- **ruler**: Abaqa, Ilkhan. Check: verified. Evidence: Iranica 'Abaqa' (opened): enthroned 19 June 1265 and died at Hamadan on 1 April 1282; he also campaigned in Khorasan in 1279. Cited: Abaqa, Peter Jackson, Encyclopaedia Iranica I/1, pp. 61-63, https://iranicaonline.org/articles/abaqa, opening paragraph (enthroned 19 June 1265; died at Hamadan 1 April 1282)

- **government**: Mongol khanate under the Great Khan's suzerainty. Check: verified. Evidence: Iranica 'Abaqa' (opened): Abaqa's suzerain was the Great Khan Qubilai; his confirmation came in 1270, with a second enthronement, and contacts with Qubilai continued. Cited: Abaqa, Peter Jackson, Encyclopaedia Iranica I/1, pp. 61-63, https://iranicaonline.org/articles/abaqa, opening paragraph ('his suzerain, the Great Khan Qubilai'; confirmation arrived 1270)

- **currency**: Silver coinage (Tabriz type, from 1275). Check: verified; the opened source does not name the denomination. Evidence: Iranica 'Coins and Coinage' (opened): silver coinage was in production across Persia by the late 1270s; Abaqa introduced a standard Tabriz type in 1275, Uighur on one face and Arabic on the other. The article does not name the denomination of that type, but the silver unit is the dirham. Cited: Coins and Coinage, Stephen Album, Michael L. Bates, Willem M. Floor, Encyclopaedia Iranica VI/1, pp. 14-41, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/coins-and-coinage/, Il-khanid section ('first attempt at unifying the local coinage was made by Abaqa ... in 674/1275', Uighur obverse, Arabic reverse)

## Colour 5: Yuan Empire (Mongolian homeland)

State: Mongol homeland, held by Kublai's Yuan and pressed by Qaidu.

- **ruler**: Kublai, Great Khan and Emperor of the Yuan. Check: verified. Evidence: Iranica 'Abaqa' (opened) names the Great Khan Qubilai in China; Yuanshi Benji 10 (opened) shows him acting as emperor in 1279. The Abaqa article alone does not give the Yuan emperor title. Cited: Abaqa, Peter Jackson, Encyclopaedia Iranica I/1, pp. 61-63, https://iranicaonline.org/articles/abaqa, opening paragraph ('the Great Khan Qubilai in China')

- **government**: Imperial monarchy under a Mongol dynasty. Check: verified. Evidence: Yuanshi Benji 7 (opened): the Great Yuan dynastic name was founded in the eleventh month of Zhiyuan 8 (1271). Cited: Yuanshi, Benji 7: Shizu 4, 8th year of Zhiyuan (1271), eleventh month: edict founding the dynastic name Great Yuan, Song Lian et al. (compilers), https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/元史/卷007, 本紀第七 世祖四, 至元八年十一月

- **demonym**: Mongol. Check: verified. Evidence: Iranica 'Mongols' (opened): the Mongols are a people whose home lay in the east of modern Mongolia. Cited: Mongols, Peter Jackson, Encyclopaedia Iranica, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/mongols/, opening sentence ('MONGOLS, an Altaic people whose home lay in the east of the modern republic of Mongolia')

## Colour 6: Goryeo (Korea)

State: Kingdom of Goryeo under King Chungnyeol, a Yuan vassal and imperial son-in-law.

- **ruler**: Chungnyeol, King of Goryeo. Check: verified. Evidence: Goryeosa 28 (opened): Chungnyeol succeeded in 1274; Goryeosa 29 (opened): in his 5th year (1279) he is at the Yuan court, then returns, still king. Cited: Goryeosa (History of Goryeo), Sega 28: King Chungnyeol 1, and Sega 29: King Chungnyeol 2, 5th year (1279), Jeong Inji, Kim Jongseo et al. (compilers), https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/高麗史/卷二十八 ; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/高麗史/卷二十九, 世家卷第二十八 忠烈王一 (accession in Wonjong's 15th year, 1274); 卷二十九 忠烈王二, 五年 (己卯, 1279: the king is at the Yuan court and returns)

- **government**: Kingdom under Yuan suzerainty. Check: verified. Evidence: Goryeosa 28 (opened): he married Kublai's daughter and a Yuan envoy addressed him as the emperor's son-in-law; Goryeosa 29 (opened): in 1279 he attends the emperor at the Yuan court. Cited: Goryeosa, Sega 28: King Chungnyeol 1, Jeong Inji, Kim Jongseo et al. (compilers), https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/高麗史/卷二十八, opening entry (he married a daughter of Yuan Shizu, Princess Qutlugh Kelmish, and is addressed by a Yuan envoy as the emperor's son-in-law, 駙馬)

## Colour 7: Rus' principalities (under the Golden Horde)

State: Rus' principalities, tributary to the Golden Horde.

- **government**: Principalities tributary to the Golden Horde. Check: verified. Evidence: Iranica 'Golden Horde' (opened), second paragraph: refers to the tributary Russian lands within the Golden Horde's sphere. Cited: Golden Horde, Peter Jackson, Encyclopaedia Iranica XI/1, pp. 75-76, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/golden-horde/, second paragraph ('the tributary Russian lands')

## Colour 8: Sultanate of Rum (Ilkhanid vassal)

State: Seljuk Sultanate of Rum (Anatolia) under Kaykhusraw III, controlled by the Ilkhanate.

- **ruler**: Kaykhusraw III, Seljuk Sultan of Rum. Check: verified. Evidence: TDV (opened): Keyhusrev III was Anatolian Seljuk sultan 1266-1282, nominal throughout the 1279 upheaval. Khalili catalogue (opened) lists him as ruler on a 678 AH dirham. Cited: Keyhusrev III (TDV Islam Ansiklopedisi, vol. 25), Ali Sevim, TDV Islam Arastirmalari Merkezi, Ankara 2022, pp. 350-351; https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/keyhusrev-iii, whole article; 'Anadolu Selcuklu sultani (1266-1282)', 'sembolik saltanat'

- **government**: Sultanate under Ilkhanid control. Check: verified. Evidence: TDV (opened): his reign was a symbolic sultanate, the east a Mongol province; after Elbistan (1277) Abaqa left his brother Kongurtay to run Anatolia. Cited: Keyhusrev III (TDV Islam Ansiklopedisi, vol. 25), Ali Sevim, TDV Islam Arastirmalari Merkezi, Ankara 2022, pp. 350-351; https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/keyhusrev-iii, closing paragraph (a 'symbolic sultanate'; the east of the state 'a province of the Mongols'); Abaqa leaves Anatolia's administration to Kongurtay Noyan

- **currency**: Silver dirham. Check: verified. Evidence: Khalili Collections item 3395 (opened): silver (AR) dirham of Kay Khusraw III, mint Lu'lu'a, 678 AH (about 1279-80). Iranica 'Coins' (opened) also notes Mongol dirhams matched the Anatolian Seljuk dirham standard. Cited: Seljuqs of Rum, silver dirham, Luluah (Lu'lu'ah) 678 AH, item 3395, Acc. AR3257, Khalili Collections (Nasser D. Khalili collection of Islamic art), https://khalilicollections.org/islamic-coins/3395-seljuqs-of-rum-silver-dirham-luluah-luluah-678-ah/, catalogue entry: ruler Kay Khusraw III, AR dirham, mint Lu'lu'ah, 678 AH (about May 1279 to May 1280); ref. Izmirlier 2009, no. 1069
