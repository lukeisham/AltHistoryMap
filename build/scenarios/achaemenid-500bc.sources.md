# Achaemenid Persia c. 500 BC: sources for the country key facts

Every value below was found by one researcher and confirmed against a source by a second, independent checker. A field no source supported is left blank in the scenario.

## Colour 1: Achaemenid Empire (core satrapies)

State: Achaemenid Empire under Darius I, core satrapies.

- **ruler**: Darius I, King of Kings. Check: verified. Evidence: Behistun inscription opened: Darius styles himself king of kings and king in Persia, naming Hystaspes and Arsames as ancestors; his reign (522-486 BC) spans 500 BC. Cited: The Sculptures and Inscription of Darius the Great on the Rock of Behistun in Persia (annotated Persian text), L. W. King and R. C. Thompson, British Museum, 1907; https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sculptures_and_Inscription_of_Darius_the_Great_on_the_Rock_of_Behist%C3%BBn_in_Persia/Annotated/The_Persian_Text, Column I, paragraph 1 (royal titles)

- **government**: Monarchy governed through provincial satrapies. Check: verified. Evidence: Herodotus 3.89 as hosted by Livius opened: Darius set up twenty provincial governorships called satrapies, with assessed tribute. Cited: The Histories (Herodotus), 3.89-97, translated by Aubrey de Selincourt, Herodotus, https://www.livius.org/articles/person/darius-the-great/sources/the-satrapies-herodotus/, 3.89: Darius sets up twenty provincial governorships (satrapies)

- **currency**: Gold daric and silver siglos. Check: verified. Evidence: MoneyMuseum entry 607 opened: Darius I had gold darics minted, named after him, and the silver coin was the siglos. Cited: Persian Empire, Darius I, 1/8 siglos (catalogue entry), Sunflower Foundation MoneyMuseum (coin catalogue), https://www.moneymuseum.com/en/coins?&id=607, description text of catalogue entry id 607

- **demonym**: Persian. Check: verified. Evidence: Behistun text opened: Darius is king in Persia, and later columns use Persian as the label for his people and commanders; the cited para 1 itself gives only the realm. Cited: The Sculptures and Inscription of Darius the Great on the Rock of Behistun in Persia (annotated Persian text), L. W. King and R. C. Thompson, British Museum, 1907; https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sculptures_and_Inscription_of_Darius_the_Great_on_the_Rock_of_Behist%C3%BBn_in_Persia/Annotated/The_Persian_Text, Column I, paragraph 1 (royal titles)
